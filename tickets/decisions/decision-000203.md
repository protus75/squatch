No ticket. The behavior is real, but it is what the spec says and nothing shows it causing harm. `Drain._drain` (squatch/drain.py:223-225) arms `drain_max_runtime` against the start of the current invocation. `_handoff` (drain.py:443-457) passes only `--config`, `drain`, and repeated `--parked <stem>` flags to the child, so a self-upgrade child arms a new ceiling from its own start. Section 18 (SQUATCH_PLAN.md:578-581) defines the ceiling as a runaway backstop on 'an unattended drain'. It names no carry-over across a handoff, and the section 15 config comment (:1338) says the same. The drain module docstring (drain.py:43-54) lists exactly what the handoff carries: the parked set, because the parent's verdicts must hold. The deadline is not in that list. A chain needs a `squatch/**` or `specs/**` admission at every hop, and each hop is a reviewed merge that makes forward progress. That is not the spend-without-progress case the backstop is there to stop. Spend-without-progress is bounded separately by the per-stem spine caps, `drain.max_ticket_minutes`, and the watchdog. The message makes its own fix conditional ('if a runaway chain ever earns it') and gives no evidence (evidence: None). Under D10, carrying the deadline would add a new argv flag or signal field, a plan edit, and new child-side parsing with no incident behind any of it. A tombstone does not fit: decision-000194 covers whether the ceiling's arm and fire events pair up, not whether the deadline survives a handoff, and no rendered open ticket or merged Goal line covers this.

Evidence: squatch/drain.py:223-225 (ceiling armed from this invocation's `started`); drain.py:443-457 (`_handoff` argv carries only config, `drain`, `--parked`); drain.py:43-54 (handoff contract names only the parked set); SQUATCH_PLAN.md:578-581 and :1338 (ceiling defined per unattended drain, no handoff carry). Reopen if a journal shows a single drain session running past `drain.max_runtime_hours` across two or more `drain_handoff` signals without a `timer_fired`, or if the plan adds a rule that the ceiling is session-scoped across re-execs.
